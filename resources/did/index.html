Dissociative Identity Disorder

Clinical descriptions, contemporary research, and the limits of what we know.

Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) is a recognized diagnosis in the DSM-5-TR. The World Health Organization also describes dissociative disorders in its ICD-11 framework. Recognition of the condition does not mean every proposed explanation of it has been settled.

This collection is here for curiosity, careful reading, and compassion. Research on DID remains relatively small and methodologically challenging. A paper's conclusions should be evaluated in the context of its methods, sample, and limitations. The lived experience of someone with DID is not a spectacle and not something a bibliography can reduce to a single diagram.

Personal perspective: Meet the residents for one household's lived experience with DID. It does not represent everyone's experience.

Research and open questions

Reviews offer context, not definitive answers.

2024 · Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease · Review

Boysen — A Review of DID Research From 2011 to 2021

Assesses the field’s empirical literature and discusses limitations including small samples and concentration of cases among a few research groups.

Read source ↗

2025 · Frontiers in Psychiatry · Review

Bachrach & Huntjens — Recent Evidence-Based Developments in DID Treatment

Discusses newer treatment models and the still-limited evidence base for dissociative symptoms and treatment outcomes. Open access.

2024 · Expert Review of Neurotherapeutics · Narrative review

Purcell et al. — Treatment of DID: Leveraging Neurobiology

A treatment-oriented overview connecting symptoms, neurobiological research, and clinical practice. Open-access manuscript.
